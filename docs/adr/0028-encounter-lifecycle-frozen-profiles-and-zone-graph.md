# ADR 0028: Encounter lifecycle, frozen per-adventure combat profiles, zone graph and opening-action policy

- Status: Accepted and implemented in P11.1 (29 October 2026). Initiative, rounds, activations and action points (P11.2), attack and damage resolution including the opening strike (P11.3), reactions and effects (P11.4), terminal outcomes and settlement (P11.5) and full combat recovery in checkpoints (P11.6) build on it.
- Date: 29 October 2026
- Builds on: [ADR 0006](0006-original-combat.md) (AI RPG Skirmish is the default ruleset), [ADR 0003](0003-canonical-transactions.md) (one canonical commit), [ADR 0017](0017-bounded-pack-semantics.md) (closed, bounded pack semantics), [ADR 0023](0023-character-rules-and-equipment-constraints.md) (vocabularies per creation-profile pack), [ADR 0026](0026-bounded-world-clock-travel-duration-and-rest.md) (time moves only through commits).

## Context

The combat design (docs/11) fixes a state machine, a zone model, provisional numbers and an opening-action rule, and the owner confirmed that AI RPG Skirmish is a default that adventures may customize within supported bounds, without new schedulers. Before P11.1 nothing in the engine represented an encounter: the `encounter` pack kind was refused (`UNSUPPORTED_KIND`), `campaign.mode` could hold `ENCOUNTER` but nothing set it, and packs use their own attribute and resource vocabularies, so the default formulas (written over Force, Finesse, Insight, Resolve, Health and Focus) had nothing to read.

## Decision

1. **A profile is kernel plus parameters.** `CombatProfile` (pure, `org.rpgengine.rules`) separates the confirmed structure from the provisional numbers. The kernel (`skirmish-initiative-rounds/1`: one initiative-ordered round scheduler, the zone-graph space, initiative `2d10 + finesse + insight` rolled once with stable tie-breaks, 2d10 checks, reaction depth one) is fixed by the engine build. The 25 parameters of the docs/11 section 17 table (action points, reactions per round, round seconds, round cap, Guard and Resistance bases, caps, bonuses, strong-hit margin and die, range edges, speech per activation, terrain multiplier and every action cost) have the documented default and an inclusive bound in one table, shared by the pack compiler and the runtime.
2. **Customization is a pack rule.** A `rule` of `ruleType: combat` (at most one per pack) maps the four roles to the pack's attributes, names the pack resource used as Health and optionally Focus, and may override any parameter inside its bound. `scheduler` and `space` may be written but only with the one supported value; any other scheduler, spatial model or unknown parameter (for example `reactionDepth`) is a located `SCHEMA` error, because it would change the kernel. Cross-parameter invariants are `CONSTRAINT_CONFLICT`: every action cost fits one activation, a difficult edge stays traversable in one activation, and the Guard and partial-cover bonuses fit the situational Guard cap.
3. **Encounter templates are a pack kind.** An `encounter` definition names the location where it can start, 1..16 zones (tags, cover `none`/`partial`/`full`, visibility `clear`/`obscured`, optional capacity), directed edges (two-way by default; `difficult`, `blocked`), the player's starting zone, escape zones, 1..8 participants with a side (`ally`/`hostile`), a starting zone, an optional NPC they portray, a threat tier and explicit statistics (Health 1..999, Focus, Guard, Resistance, armor up to the profile's `armorMax`, the four roles 0..6), 1..8 objectives and an opening policy. The compiler requires the pack's combat rule and checks feasibility with located diagnostics: a connected space, escape and objective zones that differ from the start and are reachable along unblocked edges, starting capacity, at least one hostile, at most party size plus two hostiles (docs/12 section 4), a survival length within the round cap, and a protect objective naming an ally.
4. **The zone graph has two relations.** `ZoneGraph` (pure) derives range from adjacency that ignores direction and blocking (a closed gate still separates two neighbouring zones by one band), and movement from unblocked directed edges priced by the frozen profile (`moveCost`, times `difficultTerrainMultiplier` on a difficult edge). It offers distance, single-edge step cost, cheapest travel cost and reachable zones; zone distance is never meters.
5. **Objectives are predicates, not prose.** `defeatHostiles`, `escape`, `reachZone`, `survive` (rounds) and `protect` (an ally) evaluate to pending, met or failed over mechanical facts only; a defeated player fails every objective. P11.1 freezes and evaluates them; turning statuses into an outcome is P11.5.
6. **The lifecycle is the docs/11 diagram, enforced twice.** `EncounterPhase` lists every phase and exactly the arrows of the diagram. `PROPOSED`, `VALIDATED` and `REJECTED` describe a candidate that is never stored; a stored encounter starts at `ENTERING` in round 0. V22 repeats the table as a trigger: every update follows one arrow, the round grows by one exactly when a round opens, and an opening strike moves from `PENDING` to `RESOLVED` once.
7. **Entry is an explicit intention that freezes everything.** `ENGAGE {encounterId, openingStrike?}` is legal when the template's location is the player's location, its pack is the player's vocabulary pack, the player has every mapped role attribute and Health above its minimum, no encounter is active, and an opening strike is only declared when the policy allows one against a hostile participant. Every refusal is `ENCOUNTER_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 422), so a refusal does not confirm that an encounter exists elsewhere. The resolver freezes the profile and template with the player's committed facts (`FrozenEncounter`): the player's Guard is `guardBase + finesse`, Resistance `resistanceBase + resolve`, initiative modifier `finesse + insight`, Health and Focus their actual pools, and the worn items and active conditions are recorded as they are. Template participants keep their explicit statistics. Profile and definition are stored as canonical JSON with their SHA-256 beside the content revision and ruleset hash, so a later content revision or retuned default cannot change a started encounter.
8. **Entry resolves nothing.** The `ENGAGE` commit advances the game turn and moves the campaign into encounter mode with one public `ENCOUNTER_STARTED` event; it takes no world time, draws no die and changes no pool or item. The commit refuses a plan that enters encounter mode without storing an encounter, and a second active encounter of the campaign violates a partial unique index.
9. **The opening-action policy decides where the opening strike lands.** `initiative` (default): the strike is held as the initiator's first action and resolves at their own initiative slot in round one, paying its action points from that activation. `first`: it resolves in a dedicated opening slot before round one's first activation, still paid from the initiator's round-one activation (surprise moves the blow, it never adds one). `forbidden`: the encounter cannot be opened by an attack. P11.1 stores the policy, the target and a `PENDING` status; the attack can therefore never land once in narrative mode and again on the first tactical turn.
10. **Same actor in both modes.** The player's participant row is backed by the campaign actor: Health and Focus stay in `actor_resource`, as in exploration. A template participant keeps encounter-local Health and Focus in its row. Participant status (`ACTIVE`, `DEFEATED`, `ESCAPED`, `SURRENDERED`) and zone are the mutable tactical state later tasks change; the frozen profile never changes.

## Alternatives considered

- Hard-coding Force, Finesse, Insight, Resolve, Health and Focus as engine attributes: rejected; packs own their vocabularies (ADR 0023), and the role mapping keeps the default formulas usable for any of them.
- Letting packs choose another scheduler or a grid by name: rejected; docs/11 section 17 makes these structural, so they need reviewed engine code and a superseding ADR.
- Rolling initiative at entry: deferred to P11.2, which owns initiative, rounds and activations; entry stores everything initiative needs (the modifiers, the roster order and the frozen profile).
- Creating campaign actors for template creatures: rejected for now; an encounter-local participant with frozen statistics keeps creatures out of exploration state until loot and capture policies exist (P13.4).
- Storing a `Rejected` candidate row: rejected; a refused entry writes nothing, and generated proposals (P13) will have their own admission record.
- Applying the opening strike at entry, before initiative: rejected by docs/11 section 3; it would resolve outside the scheduler.

## Consequences

- V22 adds `encounter` and `encounter_participant` with frozen-column, row-version, lifecycle, no-delete and one-active-encounter guards. The backup tool covers schema version 22. The V16 operational checkpoint still writes `"encounter": null`; including the encounter cursor needs a new checkpoint snapshot version and is P11.6.
- The pack contract gains `ruleType: combat` and the `encounter` kind (zones, participants and objectives carry localizable names). Existing packs are unchanged; a pack without encounters needs no combat rule.
- `CommandRuleCatalog` gains `combat`, `ResolvedCommit` an optional `EncounterEntry`, the resolver snapshot the player's worn items, and the receipt an `encounter` summary for an entry. One new command (`ENGAGE`), one refusal code (`COMMAND_ENCOUNTER_UNAVAILABLE`) with reviewed English and Italian wording (the Italian still needs independent human review) and one event type with EN/IT statements.
- Once in encounter mode every exploration intention is refused (`MODE_ILLEGAL`) and no tactical intention exists yet, so an entered encounter cannot progress or end until P11.2 to P11.5 add them. The playthrough page has no control that sends `ENGAGE`; the encounter panel is P12.1.
- Not proven here: balance of the provisional numbers (P18.3), equipment Guard and armor modifiers (items have no such field until P11.3), concealed participants (all participants are observable), hazards, and any browser journey.
