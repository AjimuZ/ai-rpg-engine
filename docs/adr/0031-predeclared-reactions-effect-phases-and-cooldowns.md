# ADR 0031: Predeclared reactions, effect phases, durations and cooldowns

- Status: Accepted and implemented in P11.4 (30 October 2026). Terminal outcomes and settlement (P11.5), checkpoint recovery (P11.6) and constrained tactics (P12.3/P12.4) build on it.
- Date: 30 October 2026
- Builds on: [ADR 0006](0006-original-combat.md) (AI RPG Skirmish), [ADR 0028](0028-encounter-lifecycle-frozen-profiles-and-zone-graph.md) (frozen encounters), [ADR 0029](0029-initiative-rounds-activations-and-bounded-combat-dialogue.md) (scheduler), [ADR 0030](0030-attacks-defense-damage-and-healing-with-recorded-draws.md) (attacks).

## Context

After P11.3 reaction budgets refreshed and were never spent, conditions changed nothing in a fight, and abilities could not be used in one. Docs/11 sections 7, 10 and 11 fix the structure: predeclared reactions without manual windows, Brace under the Guard action's stacking key, depth one, eight named effect phases, declared clocks, five stacking modes, explicit prevention, and abilities with costs and cooldowns that cannot repeat for free. They leave open where this data lives, how its timers are stored, what bounds keep an operation finite and which values are public.

## Decision

1. **Combat forms are closed pack data, frozen at entry.** A condition's optional `combat` block and an ability's optional `combat` block are the only way into a fight; a template participant may declare `reactions` and entry `conditions`, and an attack `onHit` effects. Each frozen encounter stores the definitions it can reach, so an effect never reads the pack during the fight. A condition without a combat block keeps its exploration meaning, and its attribute modifiers do not move frozen role values: role values are read once at entry, as ADR 0028 decided. Combat references name a condition of the same pack with a combat block, because another pack's condition cannot be proved to have one at import.
2. **Timers live on the participant row.** `encounter_participant` gains `effects`, `reaction_policies` and `ability_uses` as canonical JSON, written with its other final facts, and `encounter` gains `effects_applied`, the application counter. Instances are few (eight per participant) and always read and written together, so a table would add writers and no guarantee. V25 repeats what storage can see: shapes, the limit, no effects on a participant who does not stand, reactions within the frozen allowance, local Focus within its maximum, ability uses and the counter that never go back. Upgrading writes empty values, so without a stored policy no reaction fires and a fight in progress behaves as it did.
3. **Brace is every participant's default reaction.** It answers a visible attack on its holder at the first eligible moment unless a policy says otherwise (`off`, `first`, `healthAtMost`, `focusAtLeast`). Its bonus is the profile's `guardBonus`, and any reaction's Guard bonus shares the stance's stacking key: the highest applies, never the sum. A reaction that would not raise the effective defense (a held stance, the situational cap, a Resistance attack) is skipped and spends nothing. An attack from an obscured zone the reactor is outside of is not visible, so it is not answered. A new parameter was rejected: it would have changed every frozen profile's identity for a value docs/11 defines as equal.
4. **Reactions fire inside the provoking action, at depth one.** Triggers are `attacked`, `hit`, `damaged` and `leavesZone`; effects are a Guard bonus, absorption against this blow, a counter strike or applying one effect. While a reaction resolves no other is considered, so a counter can be neither braced nor countered. Simultaneous reactions run by declared priority (higher first), initiative order, then participant ID. Lacking Focus or being prevented skips the reaction without inventing a cost. A policy changes only before the first round or at the player's own decision (`SET_REACTION_POLICY`, free), so it can never answer a roll already seen. Reactions of another participant are hidden when they do not fire, because its Focus and budget are.
5. **Phases have one meaning each.** `roundOpen`, `activationStart`, `activationEnd` and `roundEnd` fire at the scheduler's boundaries; `actionDeclared` when the holder declares an action that costs points; `preRoll`, `postHit` and `postDamage` when the holder is the target of an attack. Simultaneous effects sort by priority (higher first), source sequence, then instance ID. The instances are read once per trigger: one applied meanwhile first fires at a later trigger, one that ended does not fire, and each line records its trigger key.
6. **The sequence is the encounter's application counter.** It orders exactly as the events announcing the effects do and is known to the pure scheduler, which does not know event sequences. An instance's identity is `e<sequence>`.
7. **An effect cannot apply another.** Ticks deal or restore a fixed amount, so effect processing has no cycle to detect and needs no draw. A bound of 64 automatic ticks per operation still stops an extreme fight: the rest are skipped with a public mark, which is safe where failing would strand the campaign, as P11.2's review found for a fight that could not start. Prevention is explicit: `movement`, `actions`, `reactions`, `resources`. A prevented player keeps its decision with no usable points, because speech and the end of the activation must stay available.
8. **Clocks count one unit each.** `instant`, `nextActivation` (of the holder or the source), `activations`, `roundEnds`, `worldSeconds` and `persistent`. An activation counts only if it started after the effect was applied, so a one-activation effect is felt for one whole activation. Only `worldSeconds` loses the round's seconds, so world time never ticks a round clock a second time. A count of zero expires at once. An effect waiting for an actor who fell ends, whatever its definition says, because that activation can never come; otherwise `sourceDefeated` decides. A fallen holder's effects end with it.
9. **Stacking follows the incoming definition.** `strongest` (default) keeps the larger magnitude and restarts the duration, `replace`, `refresh`, `independent` up to a cap and `additive` up to a cap, whose stacks multiply the magnitude. A weaker shield cannot refill a stronger one that is nearly spent. A holder at its limit of eight gains no new key: the application is recorded as capped, not failed.
10. **Abilities in a fight are frozen, not interpreted.** A learned ability with an active combat form costs action points (at least one, or none with an explicit once-per-activation or once-per-encounter limit), optional Focus and a cooldown in rounds, names `self`, `ally` or `hostile` targets within a range, and runs up to four operations: damage, healing, apply, remove. Its dice are drawn once per operation, at most 16 in all. No operation restores availability. It is not an attack: there is no roll and no reaction answers it. Forced movement and attack-roll abilities are not part of this grammar.
11. **The bounds are proven per operation.** Counters need `nextDraw <= 58`, which leaves room for the opening strike and eight strikes of seven dice under the durable budget of 128; beyond it the counter is skipped. At most twelve reactions fire per operation. Effect lines are gathered into one public `EFFECTS_RESOLVED` and one internal `EFFECTS_CALCULATED` per flush, so effects add a bounded number of events; `ResolvedCommit.MAX_EVENTS` rises from 128 to 256 to hold the worst case.
12. **The record has the same two halves as an attack.** Which effects are applied, tick or end, and how much Health they took, is public; another participant's remaining Health, armor and absorption are internal. The receipt lists which effects each participant holds, the player's abilities with a stable reason when unusable, its policies and its reactions left. Refusals: `COMMAND_REACTION_UNAVAILABLE`, `COMMAND_ACTION_PREVENTED`, and the existing `COMMAND_ABILITY_UNAVAILABLE` and `COMMAND_RESOURCE_INSUFFICIENT`.

## Alternatives considered

- A manual reaction window: excluded by docs/11, which would need a durable pending-action workflow.
- A separate `encounter_effect` table: rejected, see decision 2.
- Letting effects apply effects, with a runtime cycle detector: rejected; a closed grammar removes the failure instead of handling it.
- Running ability effects through the exploration effect program: rejected; it reads the pack during the fight and its expressions read campaign actors that template participants do not have.
- Failing an operation when a bound is reached: rejected; a fight must never become unable to continue because of its own content.
- Using the source event sequence literally: rejected, see decision 6.

## Consequences

- New tactical intentions `COMBAT_USE_ABILITY` and `SET_REACTION_POLICY`; new events `REACTION_RESOLVED`, `REACTION_POLICY_SET`, `COMBAT_ABILITY_USED`, `STRIKE_LAPSED`, `EFFECTS_RESOLVED` and the internal `EFFECTS_CALCULATED`; backup schema 25.
- A new fight has Brace on by default, for the player too, so the worked fight of docs/20 now plays exactly as written.
- Limits: item contracts still cannot apply or remove effects, and multi-target or hostile-target item contracts stay refused (`EFFECT_UNSUPPORTED`, `TARGET_ILLEGAL`), because their effect program is the exploration one. Non-player participants do not use abilities until P12.3/P12.4. A reaction policy can be read back from a tactical receipt, but entry's receipt does not list reactions yet. Settlement's conversion of remaining effects to exploration, and clearing effects when a participant escapes or surrenders, are P11.5. Ready and Assess have no combat form yet and no later task names them; P11.5 should decide this with Withdraw. Balance remains provisional (P18.3).
