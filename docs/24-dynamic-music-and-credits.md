# Dynamic Music and Credits

## 1. Status and scope

This chapter specifies P17.2 work. Delivered so far: the reviewed catalog (P17.2-A) and, since P17.2-B, an optional client music player (`frontend/src/app/music/`): public-state mood mapping, an explicit opt-in, crossfades, deduplicated cues, pause/mute/music volume, current-track credits, a searchable credits list and silent fallback. Delivered since P17.2-C: complete current-track credits, a downloadable credits list of the tracks played in a visit, a `music` credits section in every campaign export, admitted local tracks, a separate persisted effects volume and a validated mood-policy format (see below). Not delivered: any bundled local track, a pack that actually delivers its mood policy to the client, an audible effect for the effects volume (there are no effects), spoken-dialogue ducking, a page-level `media-src` policy or MIME/size/redirect checks on the remote file (the app sets no page Content-Security-Policy today, so the browser's own media handling applies), and any verified playback from the real host.

**As built (P17.2-C).** (1) Credits: the current-track view shows title, creator, license link, source link, notices, disclosed modifications and the rights-check date; `creditText` (`music-catalog.ts`) produces the same fields as text. `MusicPlayer.used` lists, once each and per scope, the recordings that actually started, and the panel shows their `creditText` in a read-only text box and as a `music-credits.txt` download built in the page. Both campaign export documents (`CampaignExports`, section `music`) carry the full credit record of every reviewed recording; the engine does not record which track sounded on a device, so the section names all recordings the catalog can offer rather than guessing, and holds no audio or media address. (2) Local tracks: `MUSIC_LOCAL` (default empty) takes records that `admitLocal` checks like an online one: every credit field present, a known mood, an admitted license with its exact version URL, an HTTPS source page on an approved host, an ISO date, a plain `.ogg/.mp3/.wav/.m4a` file name and a SHA-256; a refused record is dropped whole. An admitted local record plays from `/assets/music/<file>`, is preferred over an online record of the same mood, and then follows the same loop, crossfade, deduplication and failure rules (a lost file is `unavailable`, nothing substitutes). No file is bundled and the hash is recorded, not recomputed in the browser. (3) Volumes: music and effects volumes are separate sliders saved in `engine.music.v1` (an older record without `effects` loads with the default); nothing reads the effects volume yet. (4) Mood policy: `MoodPolicy` maps the six public states `quiet`, `offered`, `fight`, `won`, `lost`, `settled` to moods; `parseMoodPolicy` accepts only those keys and known moods and drops anything else whole, so a policy cannot name hidden state. The playthrough uses `DEFAULT_MOOD_POLICY`, the P17.2-B mapping; there is no pack field or route that delivers a pack's own policy to the client yet: that delivery and QA-32 are left to P17.2-D. Music is optional and must never block narration, combat, saves or the text-focused interface.

**As built (P17.2-B).** `moodOf` (`music-mood.ts`) reads only the player-safe combat projection: a fight in progress is `combat`, a resolved fight `victory` or `defeat` (an escape or stalemate returns to `exploration`), an offered encounter is `tension`, anything else `exploration`. Only `exploration` and `tension` have a reviewed recording, so every other mood is silence and no other track stands in. `MusicPlayer` (`music-player.ts`) keeps one current track and starts nothing before the player clicks "Turn music on"; the preference (`engine.music.v1`: enabled, muted, volume) is saved on the device, but a reload needs a new click, so reconnecting never starts sound. The same scope and mood, or a mood mapping to the current track, restarts nothing; a different campaign silences the old track at once. A change fades over 2 seconds in 100 ms steps (20 integer steps) with at most two audible sources, and a candidate that is still loading is silent and discarded if a newer cue or a pause arrives. A browser denial shows `blocked` and a failed or removed source shows `unavailable`, keeping the current track (or silence) and never retrying by itself; the button is the explicit retry. The media address is derived only from a reviewed file name on `commons.wikimedia.org` (`Special:FilePath`), so a story, pack or stream event cannot supply one, and the audio element is created only after the click. The client holds a copy of the reviewed records (`music-catalog.ts`) that must be kept equal to the backend `catalog.json` by hand: no automated parity check exists.

Optional spoken dialogue is a distinct [local speech capability](30-local-speech-synthesis.md),
not a new music-source license. The mixer keeps separate voice/music/effect
controls, with optional music ducking during deliberate dialogue playback and
no autoplay on reconnect. Speech follows each accepted EN/IT passage; music cues
remain derived only from observable public state. Reference voices, model weights
and generated recordings each require their own rights/provenance inventory.

The owner wants a soundtrack that follows the situation and can play from a free online library without a required manual download. Support a curated allowlisted streaming catalog and optional locally installed tracks. Browser streaming still transfers/buffers audio; it does not mean offline playback. Do not require background music files in an adventure export unless the owner has chosen a permitted local copy.

## 2. Event-driven soundtrack

Use an adventure's public presentation policy to map registered cue IDs to exploration, dialogue, rest, observed tension, active combat and resolved victory/defeat states. Public events and known scene metadata drive transitions; hidden enemies, private motives and future encounters must never leak through a change of music. No extra narrative call or live web search is required for each transition.

The controller maintains one current cue, its campaign/source identity and playback state. The same cue does not restart on every dialogue passage, UI render or duplicate stream event. A new valid cue can crossfade with a configurable transition (initial target about two seconds). Bound looping and avoid an overlap of more than the two transition sources. Reading Continue, opening inventory or selecting an unsent quick reply does not advance musical/gameplay state. Discard stale cues after navigation or campaign change; reconnect must not replay one-shot stingers.

Playback requires a deliberate user gesture and an explicit music-enabled preference. Provide pause/mute, music/effect volumes, current title/creator/license and an accessible Credits link. Browser autoplay denial is an understandable inactive state, not a reason to retry silently. No spoken narration/TTS implementation is required here.

## 3. Online catalog and failure behavior

Keep a finite reviewed catalog in registered metadata; the runtime selects entries, not arbitrary generated URLs. Require HTTPS and explicit owner-approved origins, MIME/size/redirect checks, a restrictive media policy and appropriate referrer handling. Opening a story must not contact a third-party audio host before online music is enabled. Do not introduce an unrestricted backend URL proxy.

[Wikimedia Commons permits hotlinking](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia/technical#Hotlinking) while recommending controlled copies for reliability; license obligations still apply. Its policy also rules out redirecting every user search to its servers as a live search service. Use reviewed individual file references, not a scraped/live-search soundtrack. Hosting availability, file replacement, redirects, browser decoding and any Web Audio/CORS behavior must be verified in P17 before claiming streaming support.

On timeout, unavailable network, rejected MIME, removed media or missing permission, keep the current valid permitted cue where appropriate or fade to silence. Never substitute unreviewed music. Display a quiet retry/source action; the story remains playable. Local optional copies require the same rights records and import containment as other assets. Do not download a remote file merely to bypass an embedding or access restriction.

## 4. Rights inventory and credits

The initial admissible catalog is CC0 or a specifically identified CC BY 3.0/4.0 work with compatible terms. Exclude unknown rights, noncommercial/no-derivatives restrictions and merely “free to listen” or “royalty-free” labels from automatic admission. Other licenses need a deliberate compatibility review. Check both composition and the actual performance/recording; a public-domain composition does not establish a recording's rights.

Each registered track must carry title, credited creator and other requested attribution parties, original/source page, exact media reference/version, license name/version/URL, supplied notices, modifications, verification date and local hash when copied. Credits must preserve required notices and disclose edits such as trimming, re-encoding or remixing when applicable. The project's MIT license does not replace a track's license. Link the source and exact license from the current-track view and a persistent, searchable Music Credits screen; include selected-track credits in portable manifests/exports.

[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) and [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/) permit reuse subject to their attribution and other terms; preserve the correct version instead of relabeling an older recording. [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) does not impose an attribution condition, but retain source/creator credit as project policy. Follow the [Creative Commons attribution guidance](https://creativecommons.org/reusing-cc-licensed-content/) and retain supplied title, creator, source, license and change notices.

## 6. Verification

QA-32 covers deliberate activation/autoplay denial, pause/volumes, scene → observed tension → combat → post-combat transitions, stable loops during dialogue, deduplicated/stale cues, adventure switching, offline/removed media, readable complete credits, license-version validation and absence of secret-sentinel leakage. Test ordinary CI with local fixtures; an optional real-host smoke records the exact environment and recording without claiming host reliability. Of these, `music-player.spec.ts` and `music-panel.spec.ts` cover, with a fake audio element and a manual fade timer, deliberate activation, autoplay denial, pause/mute/volume, crossfade steps, deduplicated and stale cues, source failure with a kept track, reload needing a new click, the mood mapping and the credits list. The orchestrator's `"C:/Program Files/Git/bin/bash.exe" .agents/ralph/verify.sh full` exited 0 after the build fix recorded in the roadmap (P17.2-B entry); per-test counts were not read. Since P17.2-C the same specs also cover complete credits, the credits of played tracks, local-track admission and playback under the same rules, the separate persisted volumes and the mood-policy parser, and `CampaignExportsTest` asserts the `music` section (`verify.sh frontend` and `verify.sh backend -Dtest=CampaignExportsTest,MusicCatalogTest,CampaignRestoresTest` passed). Not covered: real loops and browser decoding, a real-host smoke, adventure switching in a browser, offline behavior in a browser, and a secret-sentinel journey (the mood mapping reads only the public combat projection, which the unit tests show by construction, not by a leak test). QA-32 therefore stays open and is left to P17.2-D together with the pack mood policy delivery.
